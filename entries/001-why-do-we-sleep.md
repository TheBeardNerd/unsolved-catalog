You will spend roughly a third of your life unconscious, paralyzed, and defenseless. So will every mammal, every bird, every reptile and fish and fly that has ever been studied closely. Even jellyfish, which have no brain at all, show sleep-like states. Evolution is ruthless about waste, and sleep looks like the most wasteful habit imaginable: hours of lost foraging, lost mating, and open vulnerability to predators, every single day. It should have been optimized away. It never was, anywhere, once. That means it pays for something enormous. We do not know what.

## Why it matters

Sleep is not optional bookkeeping. Rats kept awake indefinitely die in a few weeks, about as fast as if they were starved. A rare prion disease that destroys the brain's sleep circuitry, fatal familial insomnia, kills people too. Chronic short sleep in humans tracks with immune problems, metabolic disease, and dementia risk. Whatever sleep does, the body treats it as a debt with interest: lose a night and the brain demands repayment, deeper and longer, the next chance it gets. No other biological process is enforced this strictly while explaining itself this little.

## What has been tried

Every major theory captures a piece. The synaptic homeostasis hypothesis says sleep prunes and rescales the synaptic connections that waking life strengthens, keeping the brain learnable. Memory consolidation research shows the sleeping brain replaying the day's experience and filing it into long-term storage. The glymphatic hypothesis says sleep is rinse time: cerebrospinal fluid flushes metabolic waste, including the amyloid proteins implicated in Alzheimer's, more effectively in sleeping brains. Older ideas, energy conservation and safe idling through dangerous hours, still explain some of the pattern across species.

## Where the edge is

None of these explains the whole phenomenon. Why must the repair happen offline, when quiet wakefulness could do the same jobs without the vulnerability? Why does a dolphin sleep one brain hemisphere at a time, staying half awake for years of its life, and why do some migrating birds get by on minutes a day without apparent damage? Why is sleep loss repaid with interest? A complete theory has to explain the universality, the enforcement, and the strange exceptions at once. None does.

## What would count as an answer

A mechanism with teeth: one whose disruption reproduces the full cost of sleep deprivation, and whose satisfaction by other means removes the need to sleep at all. If you can name what sleep purchases precisely enough to buy it another way, the question is closed.
